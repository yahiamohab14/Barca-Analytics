Line height (centre-back actions). The median spot, in metres from their own goal, where the centre-backs made open-play tackles, interceptions, recoveries, blocks, aerials and fouls, and where they were dribbled past. Clearances are left out because they're mostly last-ditch defending in the box, and so are offsides, which get their own number. A centre-back is anyone who started the match at centre-back, or came off the bench and usually plays there. The median holds up better than the average against the odd high press or recovery sprint. Player circles on the pitch and the Median height column use the same actions, so the line and the players are measured the same way. The line pools every centre-back action, so it can sit between two partners: a centre-back who defends deeper than his partner will show up behind the line.

Offside line. When the team catches an opponent offside, WhoScored records the defender who set the line and where he stood. That is the most direct reading of the line in event data, but it only happens a few times a match and mostly when the line steps up. In possession is the median spot where the centre-backs made open-play passes, which shows how high the back line sits when the team has the ball.

Open play. Actions within 10 seconds of an opponent corner, free kick or throw-in are treated as set pieces, unless the team has already regained the ball with a completed pass. By ball zone groups centre-back actions by where the opponent's last pass started: in their own third (building up), the middle third or the final third. PPDA is opponent passes in their own 60% of the pitch divided by our tackles, interceptions, fouls and challenges in that same area; lower means more aggressive pressing. High recoveries are tackles won, interceptions and recoveries in the opponent's 40% of the pitch.
